This is an excellent and well-structured project. Creating sprite sheets (or "sprite maps") is the most efficient way to generate many consistent assets for a game like this.

Here are the detailed image generation prompts for the assets needed in Chapters 1, 2, and 3. I have grouped them into logical batches to ensure visual consistency and efficiency.

### Art Style Rationale

- **Style:** 2D Cel-Shaded / Vector Art style. It's clean, readable for young children, and easier to animate than complex paintings.
- **Color Palette:** Bright, warm, and saturated, but not overly neon. Uses soft gradients for depth.
- **Lines:** Clean, slightly thick outlines for characters and interactive elements to make them pop against backgrounds.
- **Characters:** Friendly, round, and expressive features with large, clear eyes.

### Batch 1: Core Characters & Avatar Creation Assets

**Target Objects:** Main Guide Character, Avatar Base, Avatar Customization Parts.
**Use Case:** Level 1.1 Avatar Creation and the persistent guide character.

**Prompt:**
Generate a 2D character sprite sheet for a children's video game. The art style is clean, cel-shaded vector art with friendly, rounded features.

**Sheet Composition:** Create a single, large image grid containing the following elements on a transparent background:

1.  **Main Guide Character:** A friendly, smiling animal (a gentle fox or a raccoon) standing upright and waving. It should be 3/4 body view.
2.  **Blank Avatar Base:** A simple, smiling child's face and body (neutral skin tone) in the same 3/4 pose, with a simple blank t-shirt and plain hair.
3.  **Customization Parts (Separate Elements):**
    - 5 distinct hairstyles (curly, straight, short, long in ponytail, spiky). Place them slightly above the blank avatar base to show placement.
    - 5 different colored t-shirts (red, blue, green, yellow, purple).
    - 3 accessory options (a baseball cap, a hair bow, a pair of glasses).

Ensure all elements are on a transparent grid background. The style should be cohesive, as if they are all from the same asset library.

3.  **Cozy Living Room:** A warm living room with a sofa, a colorful rug, a window letting in sunlight, and a few toys (like a ball and a block) neatly placed on the floor.
4.  **Generic Classroom/Play Area:** A bright room with a colorful alphabet rug on the floor, low shelves with bins of toys, and positive, simple wall decorations (like animal or number posters).

### Batch 3: Story Cards & UI for "Emotion Theater"

**Target Objects:** Emotion Scene Illustrations (static).
**Use Case:** Level 2.1 Emotion Recognition. These are the images the child will look at before choosing an emotion.

Generate a 2x3 grid sprite sheet of 6 simple, storybook-style illustrations for a game. The art style is flat vector art with a clear focal point. Each illustration depicts a distinct scenario featuring the same two child characters from the game (one boy, one girl) with simple, expressive body language.

**Scenarios:**

2.  **Broken Toy Sadness:** One child is looking down at a broken toy car in their hands, with a sad expression. The other child looks on with concern.
3.  **Loud Noise Fear:** Both children have their hands slightly up near their ears, with wide eyes and worried expressions, reacting to something off-screen.
4.  **Sharing Happiness:** One child is handing a toy truck to the other, who is reaching out with a big smile.
5.  **Playground Conflict Anger:** One child is frowning with clenched fists, while the other is holding a ball and looking defiant.
6.  **Getting Hurt Sadness:** One child is sitting on the ground after a fall, rubbing their knee with a pained expression.

Keep the backgrounds minimal (simple color fields with a subtle ground line) to keep the focus on the characters and the action. Include the transparent grid lines between each image.

### Batch 4: Emotion Choice Buttons (UI)

**Target Objects:** Interactive UI Elements for emotion selection.
**Use Case:** Level 2.1. These are the buttons the child clicks to answer.

**Prompt:**
Generate a sprite sheet of 5 large, circular UI buttons. Each button features a simple, friendly, and stylized face expressing a core emotion. The art style is a flat, bold vector style with clear, thick lines perfect for a touchscreen interface.

**Faces:**

1.  **Happy:** Big smile, open/curious eyes.
2.  **Sad:** Downturned frown, eyebrows sloping up, maybe a single tear.
3.  **Angry:** Furrowed eyebrows, a straight line or slight frown for a mouth.
4.  **Scared:** Wide open eyes, a small "o" shaped mouth.
5.  **Neutral/Calm:** A straight, simple line for a mouth, with relaxed eyes.

Each button should be a perfect circle on a soft, colored background (e.g., yellow for happy, blue for sad). The face should be centered and take up most of the circle. Place them in a horizontal row on a single transparent sheet.
